### Working philosophy

The recurring principles across the Home, About, Product, project, and blog content are:

1. **Start with people and the real task.** Identify who uses the software, what they need to do, and which questions remain unanswered before choosing a framework.
2. **Finish the main task first.** Prefer a reliable core workflow over many incomplete features.
3. **Use evidence and ownership.** Every project should state what he personally built, the constraints, proof or impact, lessons, and what remains unfinished.
4. **Use AI without giving up responsibility.** AI is useful for research, prototyping, comparing options, learning libraries, and debugging, but generated work must still be read, tested, understood, and maintainable.
5. **Learn by building.** The site repeatedly frames broken assumptions, debugging, and real product constraints as the way he learns a tool.
6. **Keep uncertainty visible.** The VSMS story specifically values recording unresolved client questions instead of making polished screens around guesses.
7. **Design from the operating environment.** Tablet workflows, local-compute limits, mobile privacy, browser permissions, or terminal ownership boundaries should shape architecture and interface decisions.
8. **Treat security and reliability as product concerns.** Authentication, permissions, logs, audit history, data privacy, provider credentials, and fallback behaviour are considered early rather than after the demo.

## Site purpose and audience

### Purpose

The product document says the portfolio exists to position Nachiketh as a Year 2 IT diploma student, full-stack developer, and aspiring AI developer. The desired visitor outcome is to understand quickly that he can build across frontend, backend, databases, AI tooling, cloud/cybersecurity work, local-first tools, and hackathon prototypes, then inspect real evidence.

### Intended audience

- Recruiters
- Internship coordinators
- Hackathon collaborators
- Senior developers
- AI builders
- Technical classmates

These people are assumed to scan many student portfolios and look for ownership, working demos, security awareness, curiosity, and honest evidence rather than inflated senior claims.

### Brand voice

- Sharp, curious, and builder-first
- Confident without pretending to be senior
- Technical without becoming unreadable
- Honest about learning through shipped experiments
- Practical, focused, slightly intense, and influenced by a local-AI workstation

### Explicit anti-references

The product direction rejects generic portfolio templates, copied creator layouts, fake senior positioning, vague “passionate developer” language, AI buzzword filler, overly polished SaaS styling, repeated decorative labels, and content that should be in the CMS but is hardcoded instead.

### GROQ/data rules

- Site settings and homepage queries select the first matching documents.
- Projects only appear in main queries when title, slug, summary, status, problem, and solution are defined.

- References are expanded into typed skills, authors, categories, social links, and images.

### Fallback behaviour

The React app always has checked-in fallback content. If Sanity is disabled, unavailable, errors, or returns no value, `useSanityQuery` uses the fallback and marks the state as fallback. Index pages can show an error message while continuing to display the local content. This keeps the portfolio inspectable and useful without a live CMS connection.

Important limit: the fallback catalog has 12 projects, while later Sanity upsert scripts add projects that are not mirrored into `fallbackData.ts`. Those CMS-only projects disappear if Sanity cannot be reached.

## Content inconsistencies and freshness risks

These are not conclusions about the person; they are differences between repository sources that future editors should resolve.

1. **Live CMS state is not captured.** The code points to a public Sanity dataset, but seed and mutation scripts cannot prove its current published contents.
2. **Project catalog split.** `fallbackData.ts` has 12 projects; the union of all repository content is 21. ReDDone, PromptForge, MemWorker, Tab, Midnight Code, Keel, Clipper, Imposter Game, and CoreML Workshop rely on Sanity upsert data and have no frontend fallback entries.
3. **Status differences.** RoadRunners is shipped in the original seed but archived in fallback; Model Shelf is building vs experiment; Iris Auth Lite is shipped vs experiment.
4. **LAH technology/story changed over time.** The older upsert says Go and handwritten implementation; the newer fallback/blog says the team moved to TypeScript. Both are useful history, but the current CMS may still contain the older copy unless updated.
5. **LAH title differs.** The fallback uses “Local Agent Helper”; the upsert document uses “LAH.”
6. **Repository-count snapshot differs.** Original seed says 49 public repositories; fallback says 52.
7. **RoadRunners demo differs.** Seed and fallback use different Vercel URLs.
8. **Blog deletion script changes the narrative.** If executed, only the LAH article remains live even though six article ideas exist in the repository.
9. **Design documentation is partly stale.** `DESIGN.md` names Russo One, Courier Prime, JetBrains Mono, and older gold/violet tokens, while the implemented site loads Geist/Geist Mono and uses cyan/blue as the main structural accent.
10. **Hardcoded personal/about content.** Education history, leadership dates, certifications, awards, and several work-principle statements are in React components rather than Sanity, despite the product principle that profile content should be CMS-editable.
11. **Hardcoded local seed paths.** Image upload scripts reference `/Users/nr/Sites/portfolio/...` and `/Users/nr/Documents/Screenshots/LAH`, which may not match the current checkout or another machine.
12. **No checked-in email or resume.** The UI supports both, but public contact currently depends on LinkedIn/social fallback unless the live CMS contains values not represented locally.
13. **Future-dated content context.** Several records are dated June–August 2026. This document records repository content as of 17 September 2026 rather than independently validating chronology.
